## B. 4CH1 specification outline (Triple — includes "C" statements)

### 1 Principles of chemistry
- **(a) States of matter** — solid/liquid/gas particle arrangement, movement, energy; changes of state (melting, boiling, evaporating, condensing, freezing, sublimation) and energy; interpreting heating/cooling curves; diffusion and dilution experiments (e.g. potassium manganate(VII) in water, ammonia + hydrogen chloride white ring of ammonium chloride closer to HCl end because NH₃ particles are lighter and move faster); solvent, solute, solution, saturated solution; solubility (g per 100 g of solvent) and **solubility curves (C)**; practical: investigating the solubility of a solid at different temperatures.
- **(b) Elements, compounds and mixtures** — definitions; pure substances have a fixed melting/boiling point, mixtures melt/boil over a range; separation: simple distillation, fractional distillation, filtration, crystallisation, paper chromatography (Rf = distance moved by spot / distance moved by solvent; mobile and stationary phases; practical); choosing a method.
- **(c) Atomic structure** — protons, neutrons, electrons (relative mass & charge); atomic number, mass number; isotopes; relative atomic mass calculated from isotopic abundances; electronic configuration (2,8,8,…) for first 20 elements.
- **(d) The Periodic Table** — arranged by atomic number; groups and periods; metals and non-metals (oxides: metal oxides basic, non-metal oxides acidic); electronic configuration and group/period; noble gases unreactive (full outer shell).

- **(e) Chemical formulae, equations and calculations** — writing word and balanced equations with state symbols; ionic equations; Ar, Mr; mole = mass/Mr; reacting masses; limiting reactants; empirical and molecular formulae (including practical: finding the formula of a metal oxide by combustion or reduction); concentration in g/dm³ and mol/dm³; molar volume 24 dm³ (24 000 cm³) at rtp; percentage yield; **(C) water of crystallisation calculations; titration calculations (concentration from titre).** Atom economy is NOT in 4CH1 — do not include it.
- **(f) Ionic bonding** — ions formed by electron transfer; charges from group number; formulae of ionic compounds; ions: ammonium NH₄⁺, hydroxide OH⁻, nitrate NO₃⁻, sulfate SO₄²⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻; dot-and-cross diagrams; giant ionic lattice; high melting points; conduct when molten or in solution, not when solid.
- **(g) Covalent bonding** — shared pair of electrons; dot-and-cross for H₂, Cl₂, HCl, H₂O, CH₄, NH₃, O₂, N₂, CO₂, C₂H₄ etc.; simple molecular substances (low mp/bp, weak intermolecular forces, which increase with Mr); giant covalent: diamond, graphite, **(C) C₆₀ fullerene**; why graphite conducts and is a lubricant; why covalent compounds don't conduct.
- **(h) Metallic bonding (C)** — lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons; electrostatic attraction; explain conductivity and malleability.
- **(i) Electrolysis (C)** — electrolyte, electrodes, anode (+), cathode (−); why ionic compounds conduct when molten/aqueous; products of electrolysis of molten binary compounds and of aqueous solutions (e.g. NaCl(aq), CuSO₄(aq), dilute H₂SO₄); half-equations at each electrode; oxidation is loss of electrons at the anode, reduction is gain at the cathode; practical: electrolysis of aqueous solutions.

### 2 Inorganic chemistry
- **(a) Group 1 (alkali metals)** — Li, Na, K with water (observations, equations, metal hydroxide + hydrogen); reactivity increases down the group (outer electron further from nucleus, more shielding, lost more easily); predict Rb, Cs.
- **(b) Group 7 (halogens)** — colours and physical states at room temperature (F₂ pale yellow gas, Cl₂ yellow-green gas, Br₂ red-brown liquid, I₂ grey-black solid, purple vapour); reactivity decreases down the group; displacement reactions as redox; halides; hydrogen chloride in water (acidic) vs in methylbenzene (not acidic).
- **(c) Gases in the atmosphere** — ~78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, ~1% argon, 0.04% carbon dioxide; practical: determining % oxygen in air (iron filings, copper, phosphorus); combustion of elements in oxygen (Mg, H₂, S — oxides and acidic/basic nature); thermal decomposition of metal carbonates (e.g. copper(II) carbonate) to give CO₂; CO₂ as a greenhouse gas and climate change.
- **(d) Reactivity series** — K, Na, Li, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Cu, Ag, Au (with C and H as reference); reactions of metals with water/steam and dilute acids; displacement reactions; oxidation and reduction (gain/loss of oxygen, and of electrons); redox; oxidising and reducing agents; rusting of iron requires both oxygen and water; preventing rusting (barrier methods, galvanising, sacrificial protection).
- **(e) Extraction and uses of metals (C)** — most metals found as compounds in ores; extraction method depends on reactivity (electrolysis for metals above carbon, reduction with carbon for those below); **iron in the blast furnace** (coke burns → CO₂ → CO; CO reduces iron(III) oxide; limestone removes silica as slag); **aluminium by electrolysis of molten aluminium oxide dissolved in cryolite** (lowers operating temperature; carbon anodes burn away; half-equations); uses of aluminium, copper, iron/steel related to properties; alloys are harder because different-sized atoms disrupt the layers.
- **(f) Acids, alkalis and titrations** — indicators: litmus, phenolphthalein (colourless in acid, pink in alkali), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in alkali); pH scale 0–14 and universal indicator; acids are sources of H⁺ ions, alkalis of OH⁻ ions; neutralisation H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O; **(C) acids as proton donors, bases as proton acceptors**; practical: acid-alkali titration (pipette, burette, rough + accurate titrations, concordant results, mean titre).
- **(g) Acids, bases and salt preparations** — general solubility rules (all sodium, potassium and ammonium salts soluble; all nitrates soluble; common chlorides soluble except silver and lead(II); common sulfates soluble except barium, calcium and lead(II); common carbonates insoluble except Na, K, NH₄; common hydroxides insoluble except Na, K, Ca (slightly)); reactions of acids with metals, bases (metal oxides/hydroxides), metal carbonates; preparing soluble salts (excess insoluble base/metal/carbonate → filter → evaporate to crystallisation point → cool → filter/dry; or titration method for alkalis); preparing insoluble salts by precipitation (mix two solutions, filter, wash with distilled water, dry); practical: preparing a sample of pure, dry hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals from copper(II) oxide; practical: preparing lead(II) sulfate.
- **(h) Chemical tests** — gases: H₂ (lit splint, squeaky pop), O₂ (relights glowing splint), CO₂ (limewater turns milky/cloudy), NH₃ (turns damp red litmus blue), Cl₂ (bleaches damp litmus paper); flame tests: Li⁺ red, Na⁺ yellow, K⁺ lilac, Ca²⁺ orange-red, Cu²⁺ blue-green; NaOH(aq) test: NH₄⁺ (warm → ammonia gas), Cu²⁺ blue precipitate, Fe²⁺ green precipitate, Fe³⁺ brown precipitate; anions: halides (dilute nitric acid then silver nitrate: Cl⁻ white, Br⁻ cream, I⁻ yellow precipitate), sulfate (dilute hydrochloric acid then barium chloride: white precipitate), carbonate (dilute acid → CO₂ → limewater milky); water: anhydrous copper(II) sulfate white → blue; physical test for purity of water: boils at exactly 100 °C (freezes at 0 °C) at 1 atm.

### 3 Physical chemistry
- **(a) Energetics** — exothermic (heat given out, temperature rises, ΔH negative) and endothermic (ΔH positive); practical: calorimetry for dissolving, displacement, combustion and neutralisation; heat energy change Q = m × c × ΔT (c = 4.2 J/g/°C); molar enthalpy change ΔH = −Q/n in kJ/mol; energy level diagrams; **(C) bond breaking is endothermic, bond making is exothermic; calculate ΔH from bond energies (ΔH = energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds)**; activation energy on profiles.
- **(b) Rates of reaction** — factors: surface area (particle size), concentration, temperature, catalyst (and pressure for gases); practical: marble chips + hydrochloric acid (mass loss or gas syringe), and effect of catalyst on decomposition of hydrogen peroxide (manganese(IV) oxide); interpreting graphs (gradient = rate; plateau = reaction finished because the limiting reactant is used up); collision theory; activation energy; catalysts provide an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and are not used up; reaction profile with and without a catalyst.
- **(c) Reversible reactions and equilibria** — ⇌ symbol; e.g. heating ammonium chloride, hydrated copper(II) sulfate dehydration (blue ↔ white); **(C) dynamic equilibrium in a closed system (rates of forward and backward reactions equal, concentrations constant); effect of changing temperature and pressure on the position of equilibrium and yield (Le Chatelier); catalyst does not change position of equilibrium; Haber process as context (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, iron catalyst, ~450 °C, ~200 atm, exothermic forward reaction).**

### 4 Organic chemistry
- **(a) Introduction** — hydrocarbon; molecular, empirical, structural, displayed and general formulae; homologous series (same general formula, same functional group, similar chemical properties, gradation in physical properties, differ by CH₂); functional groups; naming (meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-) of alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids, esters; structural isomers; reaction types: substitution, addition, combustion.
- **(b) Crude oil** — mixture of hydrocarbons; fractional distillation into fractions (refinery gases, gasoline, kerosene, diesel, fuel oil, bitumen) and their uses; trends in boiling point, colour, viscosity with chain length; complete combustion → CO₂ + H₂O, incomplete → CO (toxic, binds to haemoglobin) and soot; sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides → acid rain; cracking (catalytic: silica/alumina ~600–700 °C) to meet demand and make alkenes.
- **(c) Alkanes** — CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, saturated; combustion; substitution with bromine/halogens in UV light.
- **(d) Alkenes** — CₙH₂ₙ, C=C double bond, unsaturated; addition of bromine (bromine water turns from orange to colourless — test for alkenes); hydration of ethene with steam to make ethanol is covered in 4(e).
- **(e) Alcohols (C)** — functional group –OH; ethanol made by fermentation (glucose + yeast, ~30 °C, anaerobic) vs hydration of ethene (steam, ~300 °C, 60–70 atm, phosphoric acid catalyst) — compare; dehydration of ethanol to ethene (aluminium oxide); combustion; oxidation to ethanoic acid (by microbes / acidified potassium dichromate(VI) — orange to green).
- **(f) Carboxylic acids (C)** — –COOH; weak acids; react with metals, carbonates (fizz); vinegar is ethanoic acid.
- **(g) Esters (C)** — alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester + water (concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst); ethyl ethanoate; uses in food flavourings and perfumes; practical: preparing an ester.
- **(h) Synthetic polymers** — addition polymerisation (poly(ethene), poly(propene), poly(chloroethene) PVC, PTFE); drawing repeat units from monomers and vice versa; problems of disposal (non-biodegradable, toxic gases on burning); **(C) condensation polymers: polyesters made from a dicarboxylic acid + a diol, with water eliminated; biopolyesters are biodegradable.**

### Practical skills (assessed in written papers)
Naming apparatus (beaker, conical flask, measuring cylinder, burette, (volumetric) pipette, gas syringe, top-pan balance, thermometer, Liebig condenser, evaporating basin, crucible, Bunsen burner, filter funnel, delivery tube, boiling tube); units; reading scales to correct resolution (burette to nearest 0.05 cm³, read at bottom of meniscus at eye level); precision vs accuracy; repeat readings, means (ignoring anomalies), concordant results; independent/dependent/control variables; fair tests; sources of error and improvements; safety (eye protection, hazard symbols, fume cupboard for toxic gases); drawing results tables and graphs (line of best fit, gradients).

- **OC3 Combustion** — a fuel releases heat energy when burned · complete and incomplete combustion products · CO is poisonous (reduces the blood's capacity to carry oxygen) · in car engines the high temperature lets N₂ and O₂ from the air react, forming **oxides of nitrogen** · SO₂ from impurities in fuels · SO₂ and NOx → acid rain.
- **OC4 Cracking** — long-chain alkanes → alkenes + shorter alkanes by catalytic cracking (silica/alumina, 600–700 °C) · why cracking is needed (supply and demand).
- **OC5 Alkenes** — C=C functional group · general formula · unsaturated · structural/displayed formulae up to **four** carbons · name **unbranched-chain isomers** · addition of bromine to give dibromoalkanes · bromine water test.
- **OC6 Polymers** — addition polymers from many monomers · repeat units of poly(ethene), poly(propene), poly(chloroethene) and poly(tetrafluoroethene) (PTFE) · monomer from repeat unit and vice versa · disposal problems: inertness / non-biodegradable, toxic gases when burned.
